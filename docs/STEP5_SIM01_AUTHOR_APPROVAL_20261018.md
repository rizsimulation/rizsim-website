## Six Knowledge Review / Knowledge Gain items

Students receive the same fixed bank in randomized order at KR and KG. Each question’s answer is tied to its fixed ID.

### K1 — Who owns the next step?

Several colleagues see a visitor asking for help. Each thinks another colleague will respond, so nobody does. Which explanation best fits?

- **A** Responsibility feels spread across the people present.
- **B** The visitor’s request is too unclear for anyone to interpret.
- **C** Every colleague has already accepted a different urgent responsibility.
- **D** The colleagues have agreed that the visitor requires no assistance.

Draft answer: **A**

### K2 — Reading the room

At a workshop, a participant looks distressed. Others remain quiet. You are unsure whether help is needed. How can the group’s silence delay action?

- **A** It proves the distressed participant would prefer not to receive help.
- **B** It confirms that a responsible person has already provided assistance.
- **C** It can make people mistake others’ silence for reassurance.
- **D** It establishes that the original concern was only an imagined problem.

Draft answer: **C**

### K3 — A clear request for help

A group message asks for help with a stranded visitor. Many people read it, but no one responds. Which next message most directly addresses shared responsibility?

- **A** Could somebody please assist the visitor when time becomes available?
- **B** “Amina, please contact the help desk and confirm here.”
- **C** Many people have seen this message; please read it again.
- **D** Let us wait for someone nearby to volunteer independently soon.

Draft answer: **B**

### K4 — What does a study show?

In a controlled study, people respond less often when they believe other witnesses are present than when they believe they are alone. What is the best conclusion?

- **A** People never help if they think additional witnesses are present.
- **B** Larger groups always demonstrate a stronger concern for other people.
- **C** The experiment establishes the moral character of individual study participants.
- **D** Other witnesses can reduce a person’s likelihood of helping.

Draft answer: **D**

### K5 — When a crowd helps

At a public event, several witnesses notice a clear problem and coordinate useful help. How does this fit the bystander effect?

- **A** It completely disproves earlier research concerning help in group situations.
- **B** It shows that group effects depend on clarity and support.
- **C** It demonstrates that shared awareness removes every possible helping delay.
- **D** It establishes that the number of witnesses alone predicts assistance.

Draft answer: **B**

### K6 — Respond without unnecessary risk

You notice a possible safety problem in a busy hall. Many people are watching, but no one has acted. What is the most useful response?

- **A** Wait until every observer agrees the situation needs urgent attention.
- **B** Attempt an untrained physical intervention before alerting available support staff.
- **C** Alert trained staff and ask one person to confirm contact.
- **D** Request additional observers before deciding whether anyone should take action.

Draft answer: **C**

## Source-to-transfer learning decisions

### Source case

A visitor at a college event says they feel faint and asks for assistance. Six staff members hear the request. Each knows how to contact the event help desk, but each assumes another staff member will call. No one calls. The visitor’s request is clear, and no task has been assigned.

Which explanation best accounts for the delay?

- **A** The visitor’s request was too unclear for the staff to understand.
- **B** The event help desk had no established way to receive calls.
- **C** Each staff member expects someone else to take responsibility.
- **D** The staff deliberately agreed that the request deserved no response.

Draft answer: **C**

### Transfer case

A community delivery service uses a group chat. A driver sends a clear message: a resident needs urgent assistance at the building entrance. Several volunteers read it. Everyone knows how to contact the building coordinator, yet no one confirms doing so. You are the volunteer leading this shift.

Which next step best applies the idea from the source case?

- **A** Ask a named volunteer to contact the coordinator and confirm.
- **B** Forward the request to a larger group without naming a responder.
- **C** Wait until another volunteer independently offers to coordinate the response.
- **D** Repeat the general request without specifying a person or task.

Draft answer: **A**
